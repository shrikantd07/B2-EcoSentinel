# EcoSentinel Environmental Guidance

This local knowledge source provides concise, general educational guidance for the EcoSentinel demo. It is not a substitute for local standards, laboratory analysis, or professional environmental and public-health advice.

## Air quality and AQI

Source: AirNow.gov, U.S. EPA Air Quality Index Basics
URL: https://www.airnow.gov/aqi/aqi-basics/

The U.S. Air Quality Index communicates outdoor air quality and associated health concern. Higher AQI values indicate greater air pollution and greater health concern. AirNow classifies AQI from 0 to 50 as Good, 51 to 100 as Moderate, 101 to 150 as Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups, 151 to 200 as Unhealthy, 201 to 300 as Very Unhealthy, and 301 or higher as Hazardous. When AQI is above 100, health effects become more likely, first for sensitive groups and then for the general population as the value rises. A high AQI reading should prompt attention to current conditions and applicable local health guidance.

## Water pH

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School: pH and Water
URL: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/ph-and-water

pH describes how acidic or basic water is. The pH scale commonly ranges from 0 to 14, with 7 neutral; values below 7 are acidic and values above 7 are basic. pH is an important water-quality measurement, but interpreting a reading requires context such as the water body, local baseline, sampling method, and applicable regulatory or ecological criteria.

## Dissolved oxygen

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School: Dissolved Oxygen and Water
URL: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/dissolved-oxygen-and-water

Dissolved oxygen is the amount of oxygen dissolved in water and indicates oxygen available to aquatic organisms. Flow, temperature, photosynthesis, and decomposition of organic matter can affect dissolved oxygen. Low dissolved oxygen can stress aquatic life, but interpretation should account for temperature, salinity, season, and the characteristics of the water body.

## Turbidity

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School: Turbidity and Water
URL: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/turbidity-and-water

Turbidity describes the cloudiness or lack of clarity of water caused by suspended material. It is an important water-quality indicator. A high reading can be associated with sediment or other suspended matter, but the meaning depends on the sampling location, recent weather, natural conditions, and local water-quality criteria.

## Waste and litter management

Source: U.S. EPA, Sustainable Materials Management: Non-Hazardous Materials and Waste Management Hierarchy
URL: https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-materials-management-non-hazardous-materials-and-waste-management-hierarchy

The EPA waste-management hierarchy places source reduction and reuse above recycling and composting, followed by energy recovery, treatment, and disposal. Reducing waste at the source and reusing materials can reduce the amount of waste entering the waste stream. Local collection rules and the material type should guide specific cleanup, reuse, recycling, composting, or disposal actions.
